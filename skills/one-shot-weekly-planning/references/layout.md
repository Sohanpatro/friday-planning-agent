# Layout: how the report looks on the page

The report is read once, by a busy person, and a wall of text reads as overwhelming even when every sentence is good. The fix is space, not fewer words: size, bold, italics, bullets and a few plain signs, each doing one job. It should feel utilitarian, like a well-run status board, never cute.

## Size does the sorting

- `#` for the report title only.
- `##` for the six section headings, each short: one sign, the number, the name. `## 📅 4. Your week`. Any explanation that would follow a colon goes on the section's first line instead, in italics, as a sentence that makes sense on its own.
- `###` for the parts inside a section.
- Never skip a level. Never use bold as a stand-in for a heading.

## Bold means one thing: what to act on or remember

- Bold only an action, a date or deadline they must hit, a decision, or the single fact a skimmer must not miss.
- Never bold a whole paragraph, and never bold for tone. A fully bolded paragraph is a failure.
- A bold label at the start of a bullet or a paragraph ("**The clocks you set.** ...") is how the eye finds the item; that is allowed.
- No paragraph carries more than one bold phrase, unless it is a bullet label.

## Italic means one thing: why, or context

- Italic only a reason, a caveat, a reassurance, a source note, or the person's own words quoted back.
- A skimmer can skip every italic and still know what to do.

## Space between ideas

- A blank line between every paragraph, every list, every table and every heading, and always a blank line before a list starts.
- No paragraph over about 60 words. Split a longer one at a natural break.
- Never two long paragraphs back to back. Between them goes a list, a table, a heading or a one-line paragraph.
- A horizontal rule (`---`) only between the six sections, never inside one.

## Bullets for separate items, prose for reasoning

- Three or more separate items become bullets, each starting with a bold label of one to four words. The levels of the plan (year, month, this week) and the clocks are separate items: they are bullets.
- Reasoning, a because, a therefore, a cost, stays prose.
- One level of bullets. No nested bullets.

## Tables

- A table only where the reader compares across rows: the conflicts (what is fighting what, the call, the cost), the calendar changes, the hours, the matrix.
- Every table has real column names; no blank header rows. Left-aligned text.

## Signs, not decoration

- One sign per section heading, so the reader knows what the section is for before reading its words: 📍 where you are · ⏪ last week · 🎯 the Win · 📅 calendar · ✅ tasks · 🧭 going in.
- In the body, only two: ⚠️ for a real risk or conflict the reader must act on, and 🔒 for time to protect. At most one per item.
- Never faces, hands, sparkles, fire, celebration, hearts, rockets or stars.
- An emoji inside the person's own event or task title is a quote of their data; keep it as it is.

## Don't break what works

- The header block (title, who it's for and when it was built, sources, "Nothing here has been written...") stays at the top, the lines under the title in italics.
- The go-deeper line stays last.
