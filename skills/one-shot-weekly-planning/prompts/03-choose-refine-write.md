# Step 3: The choice, the refine loop, the writes

**Purpose.** Let the person decide, and only then touch their systems. Three endings, all of them good: they fix it with you, they approve it, or they keep it as advice and you change nothing.

## 0. If they ask to go deeper

If they name a section, go deeper on that section only, in the chat, from the working document and the indexes: the reasoning you cut, the fuller list, the sources. Same voice, same rule that nothing unsourced is said. Then offer the choice again, word for word. Going deeper never changes the plan.

## A. The refine loop

If it isn't right, ask up to five questions, one at a time, each aimed at finding what felt wrong. Not "which decision do you want to flip," but open questions that invite them to think out loud about the sections and calls that didn't sit right: "Something in the Win didn't land. Tell me what you'd have chosen instead and why, and what I got wrong about the week that led me there." Each carries your best guess at what's off, in parentheses, drawn from the decisions you made for them, since those are where you were guessing. Never the five at once; each next question comes from the last answer. Shorthand counts as an answer: kill, push Thursday, keep, protect.

Under every question, the bold heading **"What I need from you now"** and one plain sentence saying what to answer.

Then re-present only what changed, in the chat and in the document, with the list under **"What approve would write"** brought up to date, and offer the choice again, word for word as in the build step, under **"You get to choose what happens next."** Loop until they say approve or advice only.

## B. Write, on approve

Write exactly the list they saw under "What approve would write," and nothing that was not on it. Launch every writer in one message, each spawned with the model named `sonnet`. Calendar writers create and rename the approved blocks; anything with attendees is confirmed with the person first, one at a time. Task writers add tags only; no dates moved, nothing completed, unless the person said so. Read the receipts, spot-check against the plan, report exactly what was written and what failed. Partial is reported as partial.

With a fixture there is nothing to write to: list what you would have written, marked "would execute," and close.

## B2. Advice only

If they say advice only, write nothing to their calendar or tasks, and do not ask them to reconsider. Say in one line that nothing was changed, tell them where the plan is saved, and go to the close.

## C. Close

Save the final plan and the working document with the week's dates in their names so next week's postmortem can find them. Write any durable facts learned to `references/user-canon.md`. Then five lines: what was written, what couldn't be and why, and the one sentence they should carry into the week.
